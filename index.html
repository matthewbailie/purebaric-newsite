Research & case studies*

Independent research on hyperbaric therapy.

Study

Mild vs high pressure: a similar oxidative response

International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2023. Fourteen healthy adults breathed 100% oxygen by mask for 60 minutes at 1.4 or 2.5 ATA. The authors reported a similar rise in oxidative and antioxidant markers at both pressures, while some inflammation-related markers rose more at the higher pressure. Small study.

View study → PDF

Hyperbaric air mobilizes stem cells

Frontiers in Neurology, 2023. Ten healthy adults had ten 90-minute sessions of room air at 1.27 ATA. The authors reported a rise in circulating stem progenitor cells. Small study with no separate control group.

View study → PDF

mHBOT for oxidative stress and fatigue

Health, 2011. Fifteen healthy adults had one 40-minute session of room air at 1.3 ATA. The authors reported a lower oxidative stress marker and lower self-rated fatigue. Single session, no control group.

View study → PDF

Healing brain function after 10 treatments

A physician's write-up, not a peer-reviewed paper. Nine patients had ten one-hour sessions at 1.3 ATA, with brain blood-flow scans before and after. No control group.

View study → PDF

HBOT for TBI, CTE & PTSD

Medical Gas Research, 2015. A commentary, meaning one physician's opinion piece on clinical hyperbaric oxygen therapy. It is not a study and reports no new data.

View study → PDF

HBOT for chronic Lyme

Journal of the Chinese Medical Association, 2014. A case report on one patient who had 30 hospital sessions of 100% oxygen at 2.5 ATA alongside medical care. One case cannot show cause and effect.

View study → Image

HBOT for autism

BMC Pediatrics, 2009. A controlled trial in 62 children, 40 one-hour sessions at 1.3 atm with 24% oxygen, run under medical supervision. The authors reported better scores on several behavior scales than the control group.

View chart → PubMed

Peer-reviewed HBOT study

Journal of Clinical Psychiatry, 2024. A sham-controlled trial in 56 male veterans, 60 clinic sessions of 100% oxygen at 2 ATA. The authors reported lower symptom scores than the sham group.
