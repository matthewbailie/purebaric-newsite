# Phase 4. Legal and regulatory check

Two Opus agents in parallel, 60 searches each, primary sources only, every unverified item tagged `[not verified]`, dated access, "research, not legal advice" on every file. Then write the one-page summary yourself.

## 4a. State memo (`legal_state_<state>.md`)
Nine topics, each with a bucket (Blocker / Requirement to handle / No issue found / Unresolved) and citations:
1. Hyperbaric or wellness-facility licensing and the practice-of-medicine definition (does "professes to treat" catch marketing).
2. Medical oxygen, oxygen concentrators, home medical equipment or DME supplier licensing, pharmacy wholesaler or distributor licensing, and whether renting rather than selling changes it. (Tennessee's home-medical-equipment licence covered rented oxygen delivery systems and Rx-labelled devices delivered to residences; the respiratory-therapist rule caught the in-home walkthrough. Check both equivalents.)
3. Prescription-device rules without a prescription in state law.
4. Consumer protection and health-claim advertising, and the contract rules that must appear in the rental documents: automatic renewal and recurring-charge disclosure (what must be disclosed, when affirmative consent is required, notice before a charge that falls long after consent, cancellation channels, online cancellation, refund rights, who counts as a consumer), cancellation and refund rules, late-fee and interest caps, electronic-signature validity, and any required contract language. Check the state statute, then the county and city codes (consumer-protection ordinances are rare but exist), and record the citation and the exact duty. Tennessee example: Tenn. Code Ann. § 47-18-133 requires clear and conspicuous presentation of automatic-renewal terms before consent, affirmative consent before charging a card or account, an acknowledgment the consumer can keep (terms, cancellation policy, how to cancel), clear notice of when a renewal charge will occur if it falls more than 60 days after consent, an easy cancellation mechanism (toll-free number, email, postal address, or online cancellation when the consumer signed up online), notice of material changes, and a refund on request within seven days of an unconsented charge; "consumer" means an individual buying for personal, family or household purposes, so it reaches home rentals directly and business rentals only by good practice.
5. Liability waivers, minors (parents cannot waive a child's claims in Utah or Tennessee), gross negligence, choice of law.
6. Taxes and registration: sales tax on rentals and how the local portion applies to monthly payments, business tax, foreign-entity registration, franchise or excise tax, medical-equipment exemptions.
7. Recent legislation on hyperbaric oxygen.
8. State fire-code adoption and any hyperbaric position.
9. Comparison with Utah where a verifiable difference exists.
Plus a numbered list of questions for a state attorney.

Plus numbered questions for the attorney and for the fire marshal. Memo layout for both memos: (a) ten-line executive summary, (b) bucket table, (c) findings by topic, (d) open questions, (e) dated numbered sources. Record Purebaric's own chamber make, model and 510(k) number in this memo; the Knoxville package did not, and the critique flagged it.

## 4c. Blockers summary (`legal_blockers_summary.md`)
One page: a table by rental type (homes, businesses, federal layer) with a bucket and a one-paragraph reason; requirements to handle; no issue found; what would change the answer; effect on the plan. Use the memos' own bucket words; do not soften them ("Blocker" stays "Blocker"; add "no enforcement found in a non-exhaustive search" if that is what the memo says). Count the questions exactly (Knoxville: 9 state, 12 federal and local, 4 of them for the fire marshal, 1 for the insurer).

## Required statements in the market's legal documents
After the memos, list every statement the state, county or city requires or that counsel would expect, and put each one into the state version of: the letter of intent, the business rental agreement and liability waiver, the home rental agreement and waiver, and the booking-flow terms shown before payment. The Tennessee set is: an automatic-renewal and recurring-charge section (continuation month to month, the amount and date of each charge, affirmative consent to charge the card or account on file, advance notice of any renewal charge more than 60 days after consent, cancellation by email, phone or post with a stated address, online cancellation for online sign-ups, notice of material changes, the seven-day refund right), the sales-tax line, the configuration (air-only where that is the decision), the contracting entity, and the local contact details. Generate the state documents from the generators (`make_waiver.js` and `make_loi.js` each take a state entry) rather than editing Word files by hand, and record in the LOI README which statements were added and why.
