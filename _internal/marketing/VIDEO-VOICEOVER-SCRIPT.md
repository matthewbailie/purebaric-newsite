# Purebaric marketing video: voice-over script

12 October 2026. Written from the live homepage and pricing (home rentals: $729 a month, $529 a month on a year; a typical Utah clinic charges $75 to $150 a session). Wellness-only language throughout: no conditions, no outcomes, no "therapy". Every claim below is one the website already makes.

Format: 60-second spot, one voice, for Instagram, Facebook and YouTube. Second-person, present tense. On-screen text in brackets.

---

## Draft 1

[Open: a clinic waiting room, then a bedroom with the chamber]

You drive across town. You wait. You pay a hundred dollars for one hour in a hyperbaric chamber. Then you do it again next week.

Or the chamber is in the next room.

Purebaric rents 1.5 ATA hyperbaric chambers to homes across the Wasatch Front. We deliver it, set it up, and show you how to use it. You lie down, the pressure rises gently, you breathe concentrated oxygen through a mask, and for an hour you read, nap, or do nothing at all. Unlimited sessions, whenever you want.

A clinic protocol of forty sessions runs three to six thousand dollars. A year with Purebaric is five twenty-nine a month, delivery and support included. That is about eight dollars a session at two a day.

No equipment to buy. No contract surprises. We pick it up when you are done.

Book a chamber at purebaric dot com. No charge today.

Purebaric. The chamber comes to you.

[Purebaric provides general-wellness equipment. Not a medical device. Not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.]

Word count: 170. Runs long; about 68 seconds at a natural pace.

## Three personas critique Draft 1

### Marcus, 34, Lehi. Tech sales, trains at a CrossFit box five days a week, two kids under six.

"You lost me in the first five seconds. I don't go to a clinic, so 'you drive across town, you wait' is somebody else's problem. Tell me what it is for in the first line: recovery between training days. 'Read, nap, or do nothing at all' sounds like a spa ad for my mom. I want to hear 'recover' and 'between sessions'. And I need the price before the thirty-second mark or I've scrolled. The eight-dollars-a-session math is the best line in the whole thing and it's buried at the end. Also, nobody my age says 'protocol'. Who else uses it? If a guy from my gym said it was worth it I'd book tonight. There's no proof in here."

### Jenna, 37, Draper. Runs a home-based bookkeeping business, three kids, makes every purchase decision for the house.

"My first three questions are: where does it go, is it safe with kids around, and what happens if something goes wrong. The script answers none of them. 'Fits most bedrooms' is on your website; say it. 'We show you how to use it' is vague; say someone comes to the house, sets it up, trains you, and you can text them. 'No contract surprises' is exactly the kind of line that makes me suspicious, because it means there is a contract. Just say what it is: rent by the month, longer is cheaper. The clinic comparison works on me, I've looked at those prices. But 'three to six thousand dollars' sounds like a scare number; give me the per-session price and the monthly price and let me do the math. And 'unlimited sessions' needs 'for the whole household' or I assume it's for one person."

### Linda, 58, Sandy. Retired teacher, husband is 62, they research everything before buying.

"Slow down. The voice is talking at me like a car commercial. 'ATA' means nothing to me and I'll assume I'm not the audience. 'The pressure rises gently' is good; keep that. I want to know how I get in and out of it, because my knees are not what they were. Who installs it, and are they coming into my home? I'd like to hear the word 'trained' and the word 'support'. Say that I can call a person. 'Not a medical device' at the end is honest and I respect it, but then don't call it 'the ultimate hyperbaric experience' anywhere, because that sounds like a claim. The price is fine, actually better than I expected, but say the total for the year as well as the monthly, because that is what my husband will ask. And 'book online, no charge today' is reassuring. Keep that."

### What the three of them agree on

1. Say what it is for in the first line, in plain words, before any clinic comparison.
2. Price by the thirty-second mark, as a monthly figure, a per-session figure and the year total.
3. Answer the three household questions: where it goes, who sets it up, who you call.
4. No jargon. "ATA" and "protocol" go. "Gently", "trained", "support" stay.
5. One line of proof: real renters, real reviews.
6. Cut "ultimate", "no contract surprises" and the scare number.
