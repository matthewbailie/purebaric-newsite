# Purebaric: Schools and Athletic Programs as a Rental Channel

> **Tennessee note (10 Oct 2026):** this proposal is the Utah channel draft. In Tennessee the pilot is one chamber at the published three-month business rate ($929 a month plus sales tax, plus the $200 training and supplies fee), the standard package, adult athletes first; the three-chamber pricing, home-rental, QR-code and NIL elements below do not apply in Tennessee. Use the Tennessee schools pamphlet with Tennessee programs.

**Business proposal, Utah and Knoxville, Tennessee. Draft 2, 10 October 2026 (repriced from the published table; parent-present rule restored; Tennessee gates added).** Bracketed numbers point to the source appendix. "Not found" means the research looked and found no source. `targets.json` in this folder lists 53 organizations (33 Utah, 20 Tennessee), 46 with a named contact and 29 with an email printed on the source page.

## 1. Channel thesis

Big-budget college programs already treat a hyperbaric chamber as standard recovery-room equipment. Confirmed examples are Clemson [2][3], Penn State [4], Georgia Tech [5], Wake Forest [6], South Carolina [7] and LSU (donor-funded, planned) [1]. Mississippi State reaches chambers through a hospital partnership [8]. Most programs cannot justify a buildout, and no source shows anyone renting mild chambers to schools by season or academic year. Purebaric can sell that rental, with delivery, setup, training and service already included, priced under the thresholds that trigger formal bids.

The sale is wellness only: recovery routine, relaxation and performance support, never treatment. That framing also matches the evidence. A 2024 randomized trial in young soccer players found that a single 1-hour session did not significantly change recovery markers, though it did improve one fatigue score [21].

## 2. What the research found

**Who already does this**
- **Confirmed college programs (8):** Clemson's $4.9M "100 Yards of Wellness", completed in August 2022 [2][3]. Penn State's $31.9M Greenberg complex, opened in August 2025 for 800+ athletes [4]. Georgia Tech's recovery room [5]. Wake Forest's 2023 football building [6]. South Carolina's football operations center [7]. LSU, funded through a $1M Kelly family gift via the Tiger Athletic Foundation's The Oaks [1]. Mississippi State, which sends athletes to OCH Regional Medical Center's chambers and has no on-site unit [8]. University of Rochester, where a photo is labeled "hyperbaric chamber" [9].
- **Not confirmed:** Alabama, Ohio State, Oregon, Texas and Tennessee. Alabama publicizes cryotherapy and sensory-deprivation chambers instead [10]. Tennessee's training room lists GameReady and Normatec units, and its $30M upgrade plan named cryotherapy and float tanks [11][12].
- **Equipment and buy vs lease:** Brand, soft vs hard shell, purchase vs lease and unit prices were not found for any college. The only funding detail is LSU's donor gift [1].
- **High schools and prep academies:** No school-owned chamber was found, including at IMG Academy.
- **Pro teams (context):** The Phillies have a multi-year deal with NexGen Hyperbaric's mobile chambers [13]. The Eagles and Flyers also use NexGen [14], and the Dolphins were added later [15]. The Browns have a chamber room [16]. All are outsourced service deals.
- **Vendors that serve teams:** Elite Hyperbarics builds and runs in-house clinics for pro and college teams [17]. OxyHealth names athletes but no schools; its prices are by quote, with 24 to 60 month financing [18]. HBOT USA offers athlete programs and technician certification but no school leasing [19]. Princeton Integrative Health serves high school and college teams at its own clinic [20]. A school-specific division at OxyHealth, HBOT USA or Hyperbaric Medical Solutions was not found, and no published team lease prices were found.

**Rules and risk**
- **NCAA and NFHS:** Neither has a hyperbaric-specific position that the research found. The NFHS position-statement index has no hyperbaric topic [27]. The NCAA treats NIL collectives as boosters [47], which matters if donors fund a room.
- **FDA:** Soft mild chambers are cleared as Class II prescription devices for acute mountain sickness. Examples are Summit to Sea K072757 at 1.2 to 1.3 ATA [22], and Revitalair 430+ K220290 at up to 1.5 ATA, which is semi-rigid [23]. A professional society says the FDA prohibits supplemental oxygen in chambers under 1.4 ATA [24], and a hospital system says mild chambers are not designed to contain supplemental oxygen [25]. **Purebaric's business package includes an oxygen concentrator** [50].
- **Athletic-trainer law:** In Utah, licensed athletic trainers practice under a "directing physician" [28]. In Tennessee, they work on a physician's advice, consent and referrals [29]. Neither act mentions chambers. In practice, a school chamber needs the team physician to sign off on the protocol and the athletic trainer to run it.
- **Fire code and incidents:** Minnesota's fire marshal puts mild chambers under NFPA 99 Chapter 14 [34]. North Carolina cities ordered mild-hyperbaric businesses to stop over fire-code concerns [33]. In January 2025, a 5-year-old died in an oxygen-chamber fire in Troy, Michigan. Four staff were bound over for trial in February 2026 [30], and one has pleaded no contest [31]. Insurers then disputed coverage for the death suit [32].
- **Waivers for minors:** Parents' pre-injury waivers are unenforceable in both Utah (Hawkins v. Peart) [35] and Tennessee [36]. Purebaric's current business agreement requires a parent to be present for any user under 18 [50]. That rule makes ordinary team use by a high school unworkable.
- **Insurance at schools:** Southern Utah requires vendors to carry $1M per occurrence and $3M aggregate, with an additional-insured endorsement and primary coverage [37]. The University of Utah requires additional-insured status [37]. Utah school districts can draw on the state's Division of Risk Management [37].

**How schools buy**
- **Health systems supply most high-school athletic trainers.** Intermountain covers 80+ high schools [41]. University of Utah Health partners with schools including Herriman, Juan Diego and Judge Memorial [42]. In Knoxville, Knoxville Orthopaedic Clinic supplies county high schools, Bearden among them [43]. Covenant Health covers Johnson University [44]. These providers are gatekeepers and possible partners.
- **Purchasing thresholds:**
  - **Utah school districts:** under $5,000, no quotes. $5,000 to $10,000, two quotes (verbal is fine). $10,000 to $30,000, two written quotes. $30,000 to $50,000, three written quotes. Above $50,000 a year from one source, formal bid [38].
  - **UT Knoxville:** departments buy under $10,000 without bidding. $10,000 to $49,999 needs informal bids with approval. $50,000 and up needs a formal bid [40].
  - **Tennessee local governments:** bid thresholds of $25,000 or $50,000, set by resolution [39].
- **Money sources:** Donor gifts through athletic foundations (LSU [1]). Donor-benefit funding for a branded recovery room: Lindenwood's room is "powered by Hyperice" [45]. NIL deals that trade athlete posts for recovery access, such as UVA football players with Chill Cville [46].
- **Calendar:** The 2026 Utah high-school football finals are November 14 and 20 [48]. Tennessee's playoffs run November 6 to 27, with the BlueCross Bowl starting December 3 [49]. Fiscal years start July 1 (Utah LEA example [38]). So a fall football budget must be requested in spring.

## 4. Offer design

**School configuration.** One 1.5 ATA soft chamber with compressor, oxygen concentrator and mask kit, the same package Purebaric supplies everywhere (owner decision, 10 Oct 2026). Delivery, setup, service and the $200 training and supplies fee per site are included.

| Offer | Term | Price per chamber per month | Example | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **90-day Recovery Pilot** | 3 months | $929 (published 3-month rate) | 3 chambers: $8,361 + $600 training and supplies ($200 per chamber) = **$8,961** | Under the $10,000 line where Utah districts need written quotes and UT Knoxville needs bids |
| Season rental | 4 months (one sport season) | $881 (published 4-month rate) | 3 chambers: $2,643 a month, $10,572 a season, plus $600 | Just over $10,000: two written quotes in Utah districts |
| **Academic-year Recovery Room** | 10 months, Aug to May | **$810** (published 10-month rate) | 3 chambers: **$2,430 a month, $24,300 a year**, plus $600 | Needs two written quotes in Utah districts ($10,000 to $30,000 tier) and informal bids with approval at UT Knoxville ($10,000 to $49,999) |
| Full-year college package | 12 months | $783 (business floor) | 3 chambers: $28,188 a year | Matches the business floor; never priced below it |

- **Why anchor to the business rate:** Schools do not resell sessions the way businesses do, so they will push on price. But they bring heavy use and many users, which means more cleaning, wear and training than a business placement. The floor holds at $783. Discounts are given only for longer terms, not volume.
- **Branding:** "Recovery Room presented by Purebaric" covers signage, a social post at launch and a QR code for athletes' families to rent for home. At D1 programs, these rights can buy the drop from $849 to the $783 floor. College athletes 18 and over can join NIL ambassador deals that trade posts for home-chamber access [46], with each school's compliance office approving.
- **Training:** A 90-minute on-site certification for every licensed athletic trainer who will operate the chamber (student trainers do not operate it), plus the existing runbook, a session log and a quarterly refresher.
- **Supervision and waiver protocol:** The team physician signs a one-page standing protocol covering screening, exclusions (the existing Exhibit C list) and session length. A licensed athletic trainer must be in the room for every session. Athletes 18 and over sign Purebaric's consent and release. Under-18 use happens only where Purebaric's insurer approves it in writing, with a parent's signed consent and a parent present for the session (the rule in the current rental agreement, section 4.4) as well as athletic-trainer supervision, and the waiver is not relied on [35][36]. Phones and electronics stay outside the chamber, and grounding is checked daily, a lesson from Troy [30].

## 5. Sales motion

1. **Who first:** the head athletic trainer or director of sports medicine owns the modality decision. Next comes the team physician, who signs the protocol. Then the strength coach, as an internal champion. Then the AD or business officer, who controls budget and purchase orders. Then the foundation or booster club for funding. For high schools, start with the AD and the health system's sports-medicine manager.
2. **Sequence:** a one-page brief by email or form, a call within 3 days, then a demo day.
3. **Demo day:** one chamber in the training room for a day, 30-minute sessions for staff and captains, a protocol walk-through and a pilot order form.
4. **Pilot terms:** 90 days, up to 3 chambers, $8,750 including training. Purebaric takes the chambers back at no charge if the school doesn't continue. A usage review happens at day 75, and the conversion price is locked.
5. **Timing:**
   - **Now to December:** winter sports, football winter conditioning and the Ice Bears in-season.
   - **January to March:** spring sports and pro soccer preseason.
   - **March to May:** fall-2027 football and academic-year contracts, signed before the July 1 budgets.

## 6. Unit economics: 3 chambers at one school vs 3 business placements

| Per year | 3 business placements | School, academic-year Recovery Room | School, one season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rental revenue | $28,188 to $35,244 (3 × $783 to $979 × 12) | $24,300 (10 months at $810) | $10,572 (4 months at $881) |
| Training fees | $600 (3 sites) | $200 | $200 |
| Summer or idle redeployment | n/a | Utah: 2 months as home rentals at the published 2-month rate: 3 × $659 × 2 = $3,954. Tennessee: $0 (home rentals not offered) | 8 idle months: must redeploy |
| Total potential | **$28,788 to $35,844** | **$28,844 to $30,044** | $11,948 plus redeployment |
| Sites to serve | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| Delivery trips | 3 | 2 to 4 (in, summer out and back) | 2 |
| Cost and risk notes | Each business carries its own insurance [50] | One site, but more users and wear; school additional-insured paperwork [37] | Highest cost per month of revenue |

Takeaway: an academic-year school placement earns about what a low-to-mid business placement does, from one site instead of three, with athletes and families as a home-rental referral audience. Take season-only deals only when a home-rental queue is waiting.

Payback: capex per chamber was not found in Purebaric's files. Payback in months equals installed chamber cost divided by (monthly rent minus service cost); the owner fills in both.

## 7. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen and fire.** FDA clearance assumes air only [24], fire marshals apply NFPA 99 [33][34], and the Troy headlines [30] | Standard package; the owner has set the fire-code question aside for mild chambers. Grounding, no electronics inside, cotton clothing |
| Minors and unenforceable waivers [35][36] | Lead with adult programs. Under-18 use only with insurer approval and AT supervision |
| Claims creep (performance or injury language) | Wellness-only script. No injury or concussion claims. Cite the trial honestly [21] |
| Insurance exclusions [32] | Get a broker quote for schools before outreach. Confirm product and general liability cover hyperbaric use and allow additional-insured status [37] |
| Health-system athletic trainers block access [41][43] | Offer the health system co-branding or protocol review, not a fight |
| Procurement delays and net-60 payment | Keep the pilot under $10k. Invoice monthly against a purchase order |
| Knoxville distance, with no local technician | Pilot only alongside the Knoxville launch. Sign a local service contractor first |
| Seasonal idle chambers | Prefer academic-year contracts. Fill summers with home rentals in Utah only; redeploy to businesses in Tennessee |

## 8. 90-day pilot plans (12 October 2026 to 9 January 2027)

**Utah**
- **Days 1 to 15:** Get an insurer answer on schools and minors. Have a physician advisor approve the protocol template. Print a one-page brief.
- **Days 16 to 45:** Contact all 10 Utah college sports-medicine leads in `targets.json`, plus the Royals and RSL. Hold demo days at UVU, Westminster and SLCC, all within 30 minutes of Lehi. Goal: 2 pilots signed by day 45 for winter sports and football winter conditioning.
- **Days 46 to 90:** Run the pilots with weekly usage logs. Day-75 reviews. Convert at least 1 pilot to an academic-year contract starting in January. Book spring meetings with Utah, BYU and USU for fall 2027.

**Knoxville (in parallel, tied to the Knoxville pilot)**
- **Legal gates:** Knoxville days 1 to 90 are conditional on Phase 0 of `TENNESSEE-OUTREACH-RUNBOOK.md` (counsel's answers to the 21 questions in the legal memos, written fire-marshal answers, insurance confirmation including under-18 use, and the contracting entity). Nothing is pitched to a Tennessee program before those gates.
- **Days 1 to 30:** Remote outreach to Maryville College, Johnson, the Ice Bears (in season) and One Knoxville SC (first team only). Line up a local service contractor.
- **Days 31 to 60:** A single in-person demo week using the Knoxville demo chamber.
- **Days 61 to 90:** 1 pilot running. Pitch UT Knoxville's sports medicine leadership for fall 2027.

## 9. KPIs (first 90 days)

- **Outreach:** 30 organizations contacted, 12 meetings, 6 demo days.
- **Pilots:** 3 signed (2 Utah, 1 Knoxville), with at least 60% of available pilot session-slots used.
- **Conversion:** at least 1 academic-year contract, and at least 50% of pilots converting by day 120.
- **Safety and compliance:** zero safety incidents, and a signed physician protocol at 100% of sites.
- **Payment:** invoices paid within 45 days.
- **Halo:** 10 home-rental leads from athletes, families or staff.
