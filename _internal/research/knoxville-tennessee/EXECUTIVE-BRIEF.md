# Purebaric in Knoxville, Tennessee: executive brief

**Status: living draft, version 6 (10 October 2026). Updated as research continues; a final review will follow.**

## 1. The question
Should Purebaric, which rents 1.5 ATA mild hyperbaric chambers to homes ($529 to $729 a month) and businesses ($783 to $979 a month per chamber) along Utah's Wasatch Front, open a second market within 25 miles of downtown Knoxville, Tennessee?

## 2. Verdict and score
**Market-attractiveness score: 62 out of 100. The Wasatch Front scores 73 on the same rubric.** Comparison markets researched more lightly: Phoenix/Scottsdale 70, Austin 68, Nashville 60.

**Likelihood of success: moderate on market factors, low until legal questions are answered (see section 5a).** The field is open and demand for active recovery is real, but household income is lower, hyperbaric awareness is not yet visible, and the 1,500-mile supply line adds cost and risk. A small, pre-sold pilot is the right-sized bet; a full launch is not yet justified.

## 3. What we found

**Competition is thin and nobody rents.**
- 24 hyperbaric providers were identified in the radius. Only five sell wellness sessions to the public and are confirmed on their own websites (Bell Family Chiropractic, KnOxygen, ThermoDynamic Health, Sessions Wellness Center, Redeem Chiropractic). Seven are hospital wound-care programs that only treat medical referrals. Twelve more appear only on a booking directory and are unverified.
- Only one provider publishes wellness pricing: Bell Family Chiropractic at $125 for a first visit, $100 per 60-minute session at 1.3 ATA, $800 for 10, $1,400 for 20. KnOxygen (hard-shell, 2.0 to 2.5 ATA) hides pricing; one review implies about $360 a session. Hospital visits list at $482 to $497 cash.
- No chamber-rental business is based in or serves Knoxville. The nearest are in Nashville and Franklin at $1,495 to $2,850 a month, two to five times Purebaric's home rate. National mail-order rentals run $749 to $2,000 a month plus shipping.
- One independent dealer (a self-described certified hyperbaric technician) sells new 1.3 ATA chambers on Facebook Marketplace in Knoxville at $4,495 to $5,995 delivered, prescription or $60 screening required. Her buyers are the same households a home rental targets; a $5,995 purchase equals about 11 months at Purebaric's $529 rate, which is the home-rental pitch in one line. One used chamber in Maryville has sat unsold for 23 weeks at $5,500, a weak private-demand signal. Craigslist and OfferUp have nothing within 50 miles.

**Demand signals are mixed.**
- For: 6,959 Knoxville Marathon registrants in 2026 (about 7 per 1,000 metro residents), a 2,000-member track club, triathlons, a 50K, 60+ miles of urban trails, the most-visited national park next door, a sold-out 101,915-seat stadium, and three hospital systems that already explain hyperbaric to patients. 19% of the metro is over 65 (12% in Salt Lake), a longevity audience with lower housing costs.
- Against: median household income in Knox County is $74,222 against $97,494 in Salt Lake County; 37% of households earn over $100k against 49%. Adjusted for cost of living the gap is about 4%. Google Trends (checked 10 Oct 2026) puts five-year search interest for "hyperbaric chamber" at Knoxville 1 vs Salt Lake City 6 vs Nashville 2 on one scale, and Tennessee at about half of Utah's per-capita rate; in the most recent weeks Knoxville runs at 55 to 60% of Salt Lake City. Knoxville ranks third of six Tennessee metros. Its rising queries are "benefits" and "what is a hyperbaric chamber", while Utah's are "how much does it cost": Knoxville marketing must educate before it can sell. No local news coverage, Facebook group or Restore-type chain, and no reportable searches for hyperbaric rentals anywhere. Metro growth is 7.2% since 2020 but slowing each year since 2022.

**Prospects exist, and the list should be worked by phone and contact form, not email.**
- 177 businesses compiled across Knox (110), Blount (33), Anderson (15), Sevier (8), Loudon (7) and Union (4): 46 physical therapy and chiropractic, 43 gyms and fitness studios, 37 recovery studios and med spas, 19 sports programs, 17 salons, 15 CrossFit and performance gyms. The second pass added the locally owned franchise studios the first pass missed (D1 Training, Pure Barre, F45, TITLE Boxing, Fit Body Boot Camp, Workout Anytime), eight of them with a named local owner, and filled the empty east and north edges of the radius.
- Email discovery has hit a hard ceiling: a dedicated pass on the 30 best-fit businesses found zero verbatim addresses in 60 searches, because small-business sites and franchise directories hide addresses behind forms and buttons. Nine verified emails remain, about four reaching a decision-maker. Treat the list as a call list; budget sales time accordingly rather than chasing emails.
- Two adversarial reviews have been run against the list. Round one spot-checked 26 rows (9 pass, 6 fail, 11 unclear), dropped 6, flagged 43, held back 6 unverified emails and added 6 businesses. Round two spot-checked 23 of the new rows (10 pass, 5 fail, 8 unclear), dropped 4 more (two duplicates, one unconfirmable, one resort that already owns a chamber), flagged 38, and named a top-20 call list. Final list: 174 businesses, 21 marked "Call first", 9 verified emails, roughly a quarter of rows without a phone (contact form or website only). The reviewer's verdict: good enough to start calling the top 20 for a three-chamber pilot; not an email list.

**Compliance and logistics.**
- Tennessee treats equipment rentals as taxable sales (9.25% combined in Knox County), requires a certificate of authority and business-tax registration, and mild chambers are FDA-cleared as prescription devices for acute mountain sickness, so claims must stay wellness-only.
- Every delivery and repair needs a local contractor; a spare compressor and concentrator should be stocked locally.

## 4. Recommended entry model (if proceeding)
1. Sign letters of intent with two businesses at $783 to $979 a month per chamber before shipping anything. Best candidates: a recovery or IV studio that already sells cryotherapy and red light, a sports chiropractor or PT clinic in West Knoxville or Farragut, and an independent performance gym. Skip Bell, Sessions and ThermoDynamic, which already own chambers.
2. Freight three chambers: two for business placements, one for home rentals and demos.
3. Contract a local medical-equipment or appliance installer for delivery and service.
4. Keep business pricing unchanged; test a $479 to $529 home tier.
5. Register for Tennessee sales tax and the certificate of authority before the first invoice.
6. Market with Google search, one 4-week digital billboard flight near Turkey Creek (about $2,500), and Knoxville Track Club co-marketing.
7. Decision gate at 90 days: both business chambers rented at list price means add two to three more; otherwise bring them home.


## 4a. New channel under evaluation: schools and athletic programs (Utah and Tennessee)
A separate proposal (`../channels/schools-athletic-recovery/PROPOSAL-schools-athletic-recovery.md`, with a 53-organization target list) examined renting chambers to athletic departments for recovery.
- **Precedent exists at the top of college sport, not below it.** Clemson, Penn State, Georgia Tech, Wake Forest and South Carolina have chambers in their recovery rooms; LSU's is funded by a $1M donor gift; Mississippi State uses a hospital's. No high school or prep academy with a chamber was found, IMG Academy included, and no vendor was found renting chambers to schools by season. That is the opening.
- **Offer**: a three-chamber academic-year "Recovery Room" at $849 per chamber per month ($2,547 a month, $25,470 for August to May, plus the $200 training fee), entered through a 90-day three-chamber pilot at $8,750. Both figures sit under the roughly $10,000 line where Utah districts need written quotes and UT Knoxville needs bids, and nothing is priced below the $783 business floor.
- **Targets**: 33 in Utah (10 colleges, 13 public high schools, 4 private schools, 6 clubs and academies) and 20 in Tennessee (8 colleges, 5 public and 4 private high schools, 3 clubs); 46 have a named contact and 29 a published email, far better coverage than the Knoxville business list because athletic staff directories are public. Ranking: Division I and club programs first, private schools next, public high schools last.
- **Two risks the owner must settle before pitching a school.** (1) Oxygen: the research reports that soft chambers are FDA-cleared to run on air and that adding oxygen is prohibited by the clearance, that fire marshals in two states have applied hospital fire codes to mild chambers, and that a fatal oxygen-chamber fire in Michigan in 2025 is headed to trial; the proposal recommends an air-only configuration for schools. Purebaric's standard package includes an oxygen concentrator, so this needs a decision and legal review. (2) Minors: parents cannot waive a child's injury claims in Utah or Tennessee, and the current agreement requires a parent present for anyone under 18, which is why high schools rank last and why every session should be run by a licensed athletic trainer under a team physician's signed protocol. Most high-school trainers are employed by health systems (Intermountain, University of Utah Health, Knoxville Orthopaedic Clinic), so those systems are gatekeepers and should be approached as partners.
- **Unit economics**: three chambers at one school earn the same monthly revenue as three business placements with one delivery and one training session instead of three; the proposal leaves the payback figure for the owner to fill in with the chamber cost.

## 5a. Legal and regulatory check (10 October 2026)
Two memos (`legal_state_tennessee.md`, `legal_federal_local_knoxville.md`) and a one-page summary (`legal_blockers_summary.md`) cover federal, Tennessee and Knoxville rules. Research, not legal advice. The headline:
- **Home rentals with the oxygen concentrator are blocked as run in Utah.** Tennessee licenses home medical equipment providers, and the definition covers oxygen delivery systems and Rx-labelled devices rented for delivery to a Tennessee residence, wherever the provider is based ($1,404 a year, inspection, policies). The 10 L/min mask walkthrough may also be unlicensed respiratory care.
- **Business placements depend on the Knoxville Fire Marshal.** Knoxville's 2024 International Fire Code sends "hyperbaric facilities" to NFPA 99 chapter 14, which soft chambers generally cannot meet; other fire officials already apply this to mild chambers and three North Carolina cities shut such services in 2022. No Knoxville ruling exists yet. An air-only configuration is the most likely way through.
- **Federal exposure exists in both states.** The cleared soft chambers and the concentrators are prescription-only, and no clearance covers oxygen inside the chamber; the wellness disclaimer probably does not take them outside FDA's reach. No enforcement against renters was found, but the exposure is real and deserves the same attorney review in Utah.
- **Requirements, not blockers:** foreign-LLC registration, sales tax on rentals (9.25% Knox, 9.75% Blount and Anderson), $15 city and county licences once there is a base, wellness-only claims (Tennessee counts "professing to treat" as practising medicine), waivers that never cover minors or gross negligence, and the 2024 auto-renewal notice rule.
- **Effect on the verdict.** Likelihood of success drops from moderate to **low until counsel and the fire marshal answer**; the pre-sold pilot should wait for those answers. The 12 attorney questions and 4 fire-marshal questions are listed in the memos. A Tennessee letter of intent for business placements is drafted in `legal/`, conditioned on these answers.
