# Adversarial critique: SALES-REP-TRAINING v4 (10 October 2026)

Reviewed against: the owner's instruction that the chambers and what comes with them are identical in every market; purebaric.com/business.html (the owner's approved business copy); the Tennessee rental agreement and LOI; the pamphlets; the route plan. Audience: the person walking into businesses with the pamphlet. Stage: near-final, so wording counts.

**Verdict:** Not ready. Section 1 tells the rep something false about the product and spends its words on the wrong thing.

## Fix these

### 1. Section 1 says Tennessee chambers come without the oxygen concentrator. That is false.
- **Where:** Section 1, paragraph 4 ("In Utah the chambers also come with an oxygen concentrator and a mask. In Tennessee we start without it."); Section 7, the safety answer ("here in Tennessee it runs on regular air, no added oxygen"); Section 10, bullet 2 ("Chambers run on regular air for now"); Section 10, "Utah differences".
- **What's wrong:** The package is the same everywhere: chamber, compressor, oxygen concentrator, mask kit. The "air-only" idea came from the research memos, not from the owner, and it leaked into the training as if it were a decision.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** The rep tells a Knoxville owner there is no oxygen, the install arrives with a concentrator and masks, and the owner has been misinformed on the one technical fact they asked about. The pamphlet in the same hand says "oxygen-enriched air".
- **Fix:** Delete every air-only statement. State once that the package is identical in every market. Rewrite the safety answer for all markets: the chamber is filled with ordinary air by a compressor and the client breathes concentrated oxygen through a mask from a concentrator, no tanks, which is why the no-flame and no-oil rules matter.

### 2. Section 1 describes a session instead of what we sell.
- **Where:** Section 1, paragraphs 1 to 3.
- **What's wrong:** Two of the three paragraphs are a step-by-step of a session and of the staff member's 15 minutes. The business case (rent by the month, use floor space that earns nothing, sell to the customers already coming in, keep members, add a revenue line) is one sentence at the end. The website's business page leads with exactly that case; the training should too.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** The rep walks in able to explain how to zip a chamber and unable to say in one breath why an owner should care.
- **Fix:** Rewrite Section 1 as: what the rental is; the business case (floor space, existing customers, retention or a new revenue line); a two-sentence description of the chamber and a session; why clients book it; what we do and what the business does; the same package everywhere; what we are not. Move the staff-time detail into "what we do and what they do".

### 3. Session length is understated.
- **Where:** Section 1 ("for about an hour"); Section 5 pitch ("for an hour"); Section 7 ("worth an hour").
- **What's wrong:** Sessions usually run 60 to 90 minutes. "About an hour" and "a few minutes" in the same paragraph read as if people pop in and out.
- **Fix:** "A session usually runs 60 to 90 minutes" in Section 1; leave the money math on 60-minute sessions, which is what the pamphlet and website assume, and say so.

### 4. The early-exit re-billing rule is presented as universal, but only the Tennessee agreement has it.
- **Where:** Section 2, "How it ends".
- **What's wrong:** The re-rating rule (months used are re-billed at the rate for the term actually stayed) is Section 2.1 of the Tennessee agreement. The Utah agreement in the repo was left unchanged and does not carry it.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** A Utah rep quotes a rule that is not in the contract the business signs.
- **Fix:** Mark it as the Tennessee agreement's rule and tell Utah reps not to quote it until the Utah agreement is updated.

### 5. "Don't say 'not a medical device'" contradicts the pamphlet in the rep's hand.
- **Where:** Section 8, bullet 5; pamphlet back panel ("It is not a medical device"); website FAQ ("not a medical device").
- **What's wrong:** The rep is told never to say what the printed material says. An owner who reads the pamphlet and then hears the rep dodge the question will notice.
- **Fix:** Tell the rep to answer the way the pamphlet does ("we rent it as a general-wellness amenity, not a medical device, and it isn't used to diagnose or treat anything") and to send regulatory questions to Michael. Whether that pamphlet and website line should itself change is an owner and reviewer question (RUNBOOK section 14), not a training question; it is listed under "Your call" below.

### 6. Section 6 points the rep to a column that no longer exists.
- **Where:** Section 6, first line ("Use the opening line from the route plan when there is one").
- **What's wrong:** The route plan's column is now "Good to know", one fact about the business.
- **Fix:** "Use the 'Good to know' fact from the route plan to open when there is one."

### 7. The benefits are missing, and "something people book to relax" undersells it.
- **Where:** Section 1, "What we are not"; Section 7, "Does it actually do anything?".
- **What's wrong:** The owner wants the training to say why clients book it. The website's approved line is "a wellness and recovery amenity... anyone who wants a quiet hour to rest and recharge", and it names who books by business type. The training can say that much and stay inside the wellness-only rule.
- **Fix:** Add a "Why clients book it" paragraph using the website's language: rest and recovery, the same family as sauna and cold plunge, athletes between training days, studio clients pairing it with services they already buy. No conditions, no outcomes.
