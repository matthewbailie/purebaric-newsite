# Purebaric in Knoxville, Tennessee: executive brief

**Status: living draft, version 7 (10 October 2026, after the round-1 document critique). Updated as research continues; a final review will follow.**

## 1. The question
Should Purebaric, which rents 1.5 ATA mild hyperbaric chambers to homes ($529 to $729 a month) and businesses ($783 to $979 a month per chamber) along Utah's Wasatch Front, open a second market within 25 miles of downtown Knoxville, Tennessee?

## 2. Verdict and score
**Market-attractiveness score: 62 out of 100. The Wasatch Front scores 73 on the same rubric.** Comparison markets researched more lightly: Phoenix/Scottsdale 70, Austin 68, Nashville 60.

**Likelihood of success: moderate on market factors, low until legal questions are answered (see section 5a).** The field is open and demand for active recovery is real, but household income is lower, hyperbaric awareness is not yet visible, and the 1,500-mile supply line adds cost and risk. A small, pre-sold pilot is the right-sized bet; a full launch is not yet justified.

## 3. What we found

**Competition is thin and nobody rents.**
- The provider workbook holds 25 entries in the radius: five confirmed wellness operators (Bell Family Chiropractic, KnOxygen, ThermoDynamic Health, Sessions Wellness Center, Redeem Chiropractic), seven hospital wound-care programs that treat medical referrals only, and 13 unverified directory listings. The prospect re-sweep found two more operators not yet in that workbook (The Functional Medicine MD, The Osteopathic Center); the prospect workbook's "Already offer HBOT" sheet lists eight businesses to skip in outreach.
- Only one provider publishes wellness pricing: Bell Family Chiropractic at $125 for a first visit, $100 per 60-minute session at 1.3 ATA, $800 for 10, $1,400 for 20. KnOxygen (hard-shell, 2.0 to 2.5 ATA) hides pricing; one review implies about $360 a session. Hospital visits list at $482 to $497 cash.
- No chamber-rental business is based in or serves Knoxville. The nearest are in Nashville and Franklin, quoting $1,495 to $2,850 a month on their rate pages (one homepage separately says plans start at $555 a month, unconfirmed by phone). National mail-order rentals run $749 to $2,000 a month plus shipping.
- One independent dealer (a self-described certified hyperbaric technician) sells new 1.3 ATA chambers on Facebook Marketplace in Knoxville at $4,495 to $5,995 delivered, prescription or $60 screening required. Her buyers are the same households a home rental targets; a $5,995 purchase equals about 11 months at Purebaric's $529 rate, which is the home-rental pitch in one line. One used chamber in Maryville has sat unsold for 23 weeks at $5,500, a weak private-demand signal. Craigslist and OfferUp have nothing within 50 miles.

**Demand signals are mixed.**
- For: 6,959 Knoxville Marathon registrants in 2026 (about 7 per 1,000 metro residents), a 2,000-member track club, triathlons, a 50K, 60+ miles of urban trails, the most-visited national park next door, a 101,915-seat stadium whose 70,500 season tickets sold out for 2025, and three hospital systems that already explain hyperbaric to patients. 19% of the metro is over 65 (12% in Salt Lake), a longevity audience with lower housing costs.
- Against: median household income in Knox County is $74,222 against $97,494 in Salt Lake County; 37% of households earn over $100k against 49%. Adjusted for cost of living the gap is about 4%. Google Trends (checked 10 Oct 2026) puts five-year search interest for "hyperbaric chamber" at Knoxville 1 vs Salt Lake City 6 vs Nashville 2 on one scale, and Tennessee at about half of Utah's per-capita rate; two recent weeks were at 55 to 60% of Salt Lake City, but Knoxville's series sits mostly below Trends' reporting threshold. Knoxville ranks third of six Tennessee metros. Its rising queries are "benefits" and "what is a hyperbaric chamber", while Utah's are "how much does it cost": Knoxville marketing must educate before it can sell. No local news coverage, Facebook group or Restore-type chain, and no reportable searches for hyperbaric rentals anywhere. Metro growth is 7.2% since 2020 but slowing each year since 2022.

**Prospects exist, and the list should be worked by phone and contact form, not email.**
- The list now holds 263 businesses across Knox (171), Blount (43), Anderson (19), Loudon (12), Sevier (12), Union (5) and Jefferson (1): 88 recovery studios, IV lounges and med spas (after the 10 Oct re-sweep; 28 of them weight-loss and hormone clinics from a single directory, excluded from the pilot), 45 physical therapy and chiropractic, 43 gyms and fitness studios, 42 high schools and colleges with athletic programs, 17 salons, 15 CrossFit and performance gyms and 13 sports programs and clubs.
- Email discovery has a hard ceiling for businesses: small-business sites and franchise directories hide addresses behind forms. The list has 26 verified emails (25 high confidence), ten of them athletic directors from the TSSAA directory. Treat the business rows as a call and walk-in list; the runbook orders them.
- Two adversarial reviews were run against the original business list (26 and 23 spot-checks; 11 drops, 85 flags, 7 adds), and the recovery category was re-swept afterwards (54 added, 13 closures recorded). 21 rows carry the reviewer's "Call first" mark. About 19% of rows have no phone. The runbook, not this brief, is the working order of contact.

**Compliance and logistics.**
- Tennessee treats equipment rentals as taxable sales (7% state plus a local rate of 2.25% in Knox County; how the local portion applies to monthly payments is unresolved), requires registration before the first invoice, and the soft chambers reviewed are FDA-cleared prescription devices for acute mountain sickness, so claims must stay wellness-only. See section 5a for the blockers.
- Every delivery and repair needs a local contractor with a named backup; spare compressors should be stocked locally (no concentrators in Tennessee during the pilot).

## 4. Recommended entry model (if proceeding)
The operating detail is in `TENNESSEE-OUTREACH-RUNBOOK.md`; this section matches it.
1. Clear the legal gates first (runbook Phase 0): Tennessee counsel's answers, written fire-marshal answers from Knoxville, Knox County and Farragut, the contracting entity, Tennessee insurance, and registrations. No equipment ships before these are done.
2. Business and athletic-program placements only, air-only configuration (no oxygen concentrator), no home rentals in Tennessee, no billboards or paid marketing during the proof of concept.
3. Sign letters of intent at the published business rates ($783 to $979 a month per chamber plus sales tax) before shipping anything. Work the runbook's waves in order: recovery and IV studios first, then performance gyms, sports-medicine practices and pro clubs, then colleges; high schools last and only after the insurer confirms under-18 coverage. Skip the eight operators on the workbook's "Already offer HBOT" sheet.
4. Freight two chambers to a climate-controlled Knoxville storage unit; order more after the third LOI. Contract a Knoxville-based representative who installs and services, with a named backup.
5. Decision at outreach day 120: six LOIs and three installs means expand; otherwise hold or bring the chambers home. Sessions per chamber and renewals are scored at day 180.


## 4a. New channel under evaluation: schools and athletic programs (Utah and Tennessee)
A separate proposal (`../channel-schools-athletic-recovery/PROPOSAL-schools-athletic-recovery.md`, with a 53-organization target list; a Utah draft with a Tennessee note at the top) examined renting chambers to athletic departments for recovery.
- **Precedent exists at the top of college sport, not below it.** Clemson, Penn State, Georgia Tech, Wake Forest and South Carolina have chambers in their recovery rooms; LSU's is funded by a $1M donor gift; Mississippi State uses a hospital's. No high school or prep academy with a chamber was found, IMG Academy included, and no vendor was found renting chambers to schools by season. That is the opening.
- **Offer**: priced from the published business table. Utah: a three-chamber academic-year "Recovery Room" at the 10-month rate of $810 per chamber ($2,430 a month, $24,300 for August to May, plus $200 training and supplies per chamber), entered through a 90-day three-chamber pilot at $929 per chamber ($8,361 plus $600 in fees, $8,961, under the $10,000 line where Utah districts need written quotes). The academic-year contract is above that line and needs two written quotes in Utah districts and informal bids at UT Knoxville. Tennessee: one chamber for 90 days at $929 a month plus tax and the $200 fee, as on the Tennessee schools pamphlet.
- **Targets**: 33 in Utah (10 colleges, 13 public high schools, 4 private schools, 6 clubs and academies) and 20 in Tennessee, of which 15 are inside the 25-mile Knoxville radius (3 colleges: UT Knoxville, Maryville College, Johnson; 5 public and 4 private high schools; 3 clubs) and 5 colleges are outside it and not serviceable in the pilot (Carson-Newman, Tennessee Tech, East Tennessee State, Middle Tennessee State, Vanderbilt); 46 have a named contact and 29 a published email. The prospect workbook's schools category (42 rows) is the working Knoxville list. Ranking: college and club programs first, private schools next, public high schools last.
- **Two risks the owner must settle before pitching a school.** (1) Oxygen: the reviewed FDA clearances describe air-only inflation and none covers oxygen inside the chamber (the UHMS states that FDA prohibits it, which was not verified in an FDA document); fire officials in at least two states have applied hospital fire codes to mild chambers; and a fatal oxygen-chamber fire at a Michigan wellness clinic in 2025 is headed to trial. The proposal and the Tennessee material use an air-only configuration; Purebaric's Utah package includes an oxygen concentrator, so this needs a decision and legal review in both states. (2) Minors: parents cannot waive a child's injury claims in Utah or Tennessee, and the current agreement requires a parent's signed consent and a parent present for anyone under 18; no under-18 use is offered until the insurer confirms coverage in writing, which is why high schools rank last and why a licensed athletic trainer runs every session. Most high-school trainers are employed by health systems (Intermountain, University of Utah Health, Knoxville Orthopaedic Clinic), so those systems are gatekeepers and should be approached as partners.
- **Unit economics**: three chambers at one school earn the same monthly revenue as three business placements with one delivery and one training session instead of three; the proposal leaves the payback figure for the owner to fill in with the chamber cost.

## 5a. Legal and regulatory check (10 October 2026)
Two memos (`legal_state_tennessee.md`, `legal_federal_local_knoxville.md`) and a one-page summary (`legal_blockers_summary.md`) cover federal, Tennessee and Knoxville rules. Research, not legal advice. The headline:
- **Home rentals with the oxygen concentrator are blocked as run in Utah.** Tennessee licenses home medical equipment providers, and the definition covers oxygen delivery systems and Rx-labelled devices rented for delivery to a Tennessee residence, wherever the provider is based ($1,404 a year, inspection, policies). The 10 L/min mask walkthrough may also be unlicensed respiratory care.
- **Business placements depend on the Knoxville Fire Marshal.** Knoxville's 2024 International Fire Code §609 requires hyperbaric facilities to be maintained to NFPA 99, and the building code's §425 sends their construction to NFPA 99 chapter 14, which soft chambers generally cannot meet; a Minnesota fire-marshal interpretation applies this to mild chambers (ones using 90 to 95% oxygen by mask), and three North Carolina cities reportedly shut such services in 2022 (one source now unavailable). No Knoxville ruling exists yet. Whether an air-only chamber counts as a "hyperbaric facility" is unanswered, and the evidence found leans toward yes; the written fire-marshal answer decides it. A Board of Pharmacy licence question for renting prescription-labelled devices to businesses is also unresolved.
- **Federal: a blocker in both states (no enforcement against renters found in a non-exhaustive search).** The reviewed clearances (one soft chamber at 1.3 ATA, one semi-rigid chamber at up to 1.5 ATA indicated for physician offices and health institutions) and the concentrators are prescription-only, no clearance covers oxygen inside the chamber, and the wellness disclaimer probably does not take them outside FDA's reach. Purebaric's own chamber make, model and 510(k) number are not yet recorded in the memos and must be. This deserves the same attorney review in Utah.
- **Requirements, not blockers:** registration of the contracting entity (the LOI and agreement currently name Michael Bailie d/b/a Purebaric while the memos assume Purebaric LLC; counsel must settle which), sales tax on rentals (7% plus a 2.25% local rate in Knox County and 2.75% in Blount and Anderson; the local portion's treatment of monthly payments is unresolved), $15 city and county licences only if Purebaric has a Tennessee location, wellness-only claims (Tennessee counts "professing to treat" as practising medicine), waivers that never cover minors or gross negligence, and the 2024 auto-renewal notice rule.
- **Effect on the verdict.** Likelihood of success drops from moderate to **low until counsel and the fire marshal answer**; the pre-sold pilot should wait for those answers. The memos list 21 questions: 9 for Tennessee counsel in the state memo and 12 in the federal and local memo, of which 4 go to the fire marshal and one to the insurer. A Tennessee letter of intent for business placements is drafted in `legal/` (air-only, conditioned on these answers); the rental agreement still needs a Tennessee version that lists the air-only configuration, the agreed term and rate, and the contracting entity.
