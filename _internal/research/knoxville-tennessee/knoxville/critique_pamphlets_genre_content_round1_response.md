# Pamphlet genre content: what was applied from the round-1 critique (10 October 2026)

The critique is `critique_pamphlets_genre_content_round1.md`. Twelve findings; ten applied in full, one applied in part, one left to the owner. Both pamphlet sets (Tennessee and Utah) were regenerated from `make_pamphlets.py`.

| # | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover title "Help Your Athletes Recover Faster" and heading "Why It Helps Your Team" promise an outcome | **Owner's call, not changed.** The owner asked for a title about recovering faster or peak performance and a section on why it helps the team. The critic's point stands: a headline about speed of recovery is a claim the back-panel disclaimer does not cure, and RUNBOOK section 14 requires a qualified reviewer before new health language is printed. Alternative wording if the owner wants it: "A Recovery Room for Your Athletes" / "Why Teams Add a Chamber". |
| 2 | Injury bullet reads as an effect; "commonly" unsourced; "contusions" is a head-injury term | Applied. Now: the trainer or coach decides how sessions fit; some teams schedule them alongside standard care in weeks with muscle soreness, sprains and strains, bruises, overuse aches or heavy travel; a session is not a treatment for any of these. |
| 3 | Under-18 panel omits the parent-present rule and the insurer condition | Applied: consent, a parent present, and only once the insurer has confirmed cover. |
| 4 | "$100 to $125" is one clinic's $100 session and $125 first visit with consultation; "studio" is wrong | Applied in both the business price table and the team example: single session $100, "a Knoxville-area clinic", footnotes corrected. Utah ranges unchanged. |
| 5 | Capacity sentence overpromises a second weekly session | Applied: one athlete at a time, up to 8 sessions a day, one session a week for 30 athletes when scheduled across the day. |
| 6 | "Recruits notice" and "most programs your size do not have" | Applied: "A recovery amenity in your training room that athletes and families can see the program invests in." |
| 7 | "Easing back after time off" reads as return-to-play | Applied: tournament and playoff weekends (teams and schools); a planned deload week (CrossFit). |
| 8 | Staff and two-a-day rows name no use | Applied: a rest hour between early mornings, long practices and late games; a recovery hour between the two sessions of a two-a-day. |
| 9 | "After a procedure" rows imply healing; salons have no provider; "poor sleep" | Applied: spa-day clients (med spas); clients between appointments (salons); reset row without sleep. |
| 10 | Physical-therapy rows: provider recommending a paid product; two rows without a use | Applied: people who wait; a monthly wellness hour as a standing appointment; front desk, therapists and providers at a staff rate. |
| 11 | Duplicate and generic gym and CrossFit rows | Applied: lunchtime and after-work members; members who train twice a day. |
| 12 | "$33 per athlete" omits supplies | Applied: labelled "Rental per athlete, per month". |

Also applied from "Your call": club pamphlets now say "your trainer or coach" where the school pamphlet says "your athletic trainer", since a club may have no athletic trainer.

Left with the owner: whether the staff-time line ("about 15 minutes per session") holds for schools whose protocol keeps a trainer in the room for the whole session; whether to split the high-school and college pamphlet into two.
