# Adversarial critique: "Who books sessions?" boxes, round 2 (10 October 2026)

Artifact: the inside-left box on all seven pamphlets, as generated by `make_pamphlets.py` after the round-1 fixes. Reader: a business owner deciding in ten minutes whether a chamber pays. Stage: about to be printed. Test applied to every row: does the owner recognise these people as customers they already have, and can they see the money?

**Verdict:** Not ready. The physical-therapy box fails the test outright, and three other boxes have rows written for a compliance reader instead of a buyer.

## Fix these

### 1. Physical therapy and chiropractic: the box describes patients in regulatory language, not as buyers
- **Where:** All five rows. "Clients who ask for more: people who want a cash-pay wellness add-on alongside their visits, separate from their plan of care." "Clients who have finished care: people who book a monthly wellness hour after their visits end, as a standing appointment." "People who wait." "Your front desk, therapists and providers: a rest hour on a full clinic day, at a staff rate."
- **What's wrong:** "Cash-pay wellness add-on, separate from their plan of care" is how a compliance memo talks, not how a practice owner thinks about patients. No owner looks at the schedule and sees "clients who ask for more". Two rows (waiting family, staff at a staff rate) describe almost no revenue. Nothing in the box says the thing a practice owner cares about most: when a course of visits ends, the revenue ends, and a chamber keeps those patients coming back and paying.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** The owner reads the box, does not recognise a single patient in it, and concludes the pamphlet was written by someone who has never run a practice. The whole pitch loses credibility on the panel meant to prove demand.
- **Fix:** Rewrite around the patients a practice already bills: maintenance patients who pay out of pocket monthly; patients who already buy massage, supplements or other extras (proven buyers); patients finishing a course of visits (retention revenue); athletes and active patients who come for tune-ups; spouses and parents who come along.

### 2. The box never states its point
- **Where:** All five business pamphlets. The box opens with the heading and goes straight into rows.
- **What's wrong:** The rows are a value proposition ("your existing customers will pay for this") but nothing says so. The reader has to infer why a list of people is on the money panel.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** The box reads as a demographic list rather than as proof of demand, which is the opposite of its job.
- **Fix:** One line under the heading on business pamphlets: "The customers you already have who will pay for sessions." On the team pamphlets: "Who the chamber is for."

### 3. Rows that name a person but not a sale
- **Where:** Gyms, "Lunchtime and after-work members: members who add a quiet hour to the workout they already fit around a long work day." CrossFit, "Coaches and staff: your team, who train on top of coaching all day." Recovery studios, "Clients booking a reset: a quiet hour of rest after travel or a busy week." Salons, "Clients between appointments: a rest hour between a cut and a facial in one visit."
- **What's wrong:** Each describes a mood or a schedule, not a purchase. A gym owner cannot price "a quiet hour". The CrossFit coach row is a cost line, not a revenue line, when the real point is that coaches who use it sell it. The salon row describes a visit nobody books.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** Padding rows dilute the strong ones (personal-training clients, package buyers, bridal parties) and the owner stops reading before the good rows.
- **Fix:** Gyms: "Midday members: the quiet hours between the morning and evening rush are the easiest sessions to sell." CrossFit: "Your coaches: members buy what their coach does, so coaches who use it sell it." Recovery studios: "Clients who want a reset: the same reason they book a float or a massage, sold the same way." Salons: "Clients who want a longer visit: hair, nails, a facial and a session in one half-day booking."

### 4. Gyms: the strongest buyers are missing
- **Where:** Gyms, rows 1 and 4 ("Members between hard workouts", "Premium-tier members").
- **What's wrong:** The people most likely to pay are the members who already pay for extras (training, classes, massage, tanning), and the box never names them. "Premium-tier members" describes the tier, not the reason it makes money.
- **What happens if you don't fix it:** The owner's own best evidence of demand, their current add-on revenue, is not connected to the chamber.
- **Fix:** Row 1: "Members who already buy extras: the people paying for training, classes or massage are the first to add a chamber pack." Row 4: "Premium-tier members: put sessions in the top tier and give the members paying the most one more reason to stay."

### 5. Recovery studios: the membership row buries the upsell
- **Where:** Recovery studios, "Package and membership buyers: clients who want one more service in their monthly plan."
- **What's wrong:** The money is in selling a tier above the one they have; the row makes it sound like a free inclusion.
- **Fix:** "Package and membership buyers: the clients on monthly plans; a chamber adds a tier above the one they have."

## Your call
- **Heading wording.** "Who books sessions?" is the website's phrasing. "Who pays for sessions?" is blunter and more honest about the panel's purpose. Left as "Who books sessions?" with the new intro line, which makes the point without changing the heading the owner approved.
- **Team pamphlets.** "Who uses the chamber?" is for an athletic director, not a buyer of sessions, so the rows stay as they are, with the new "Who the chamber is for" intro line.

## Checked and it's fine
- Gyms "Personal-training clients" and "Weekend competitors": specific people, specific booking moments.
- CrossFit "Members who train twice a day", "Members in a heavy block" (the Open, a meet, a race) and "Masters athletes": these are the members every affiliate owner can name.
- Recovery studios "Your recovery regulars" (add a chamber session to the sauna or cold-plunge visit at the same price point) and "Spa-day clients".
- Salons "Event and wedding clients", "Your regulars" and "Gift buyers and groups": real bookings a salon owner already sells.
- No row anywhere names a condition, an outcome, or "therapy"; the health-claims rule still holds after the rewrite.
- The boxes still fit their columns after the intro line (checked on the rendered physical-therapy and gym pages).

## Applied
All five fixes are in `make_pamphlets.py`; both the Tennessee and Utah sets were regenerated.
