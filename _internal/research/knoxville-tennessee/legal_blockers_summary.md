# Legal and regulatory blockers for mild hyperbaric rentals in the Knoxville area (summary, 10 Oct 2026)

Research, not legal advice. Compiled from two memos in this folder: `legal_state_tennessee.md` (Tennessee statutes, boards, taxes, waivers) and `legal_federal_local_knoxville.md` (FDA and FTC baseline; City of Knoxville, Knox County and nearby cities). Both cite primary sources with access dates and mark anything not read in a primary source as not verified. A Tennessee attorney should confirm before anyone relies on this.

## Short answer
There is no Tennessee or Knoxville law that names mild hyperbaric chambers or bans renting them. The blockers come from general rules that catch the oxygen concentrator and the chamber's prescription label.

| Rental type | Status | What blocks or must be handled |
|---|---|---|
| **To homes, standard package (chamber + 10 L oxygen concentrator)** | **Blocked as run in Utah today** | Tennessee licenses "home medical equipment" providers; the definition covers oxygen delivery systems and Rx-labelled devices sold or rented for delivery to a Tennessee residence, wherever the provider is based (Tenn. Code Ann. § 68-11-201; Rule 0720-30-.02; $1,404 a year, inspection, written policies; out-of-state providers eligible since 2023). The in-home walkthrough of a 10 L/min mask set-up may be unlicensed respiratory care (Rule 1330-01-.03 exempts only under 6 L/min by nasal cannula). |
| **To homes, air-only (no concentrator)** | Requirement to handle | The chamber still carries an Rx-only label, which may itself fall within the HME definition and within § 53-10-104 ("any item" federal law limits to prescription). Unresolved; needs an opinion. |
| **To businesses (gyms, studios, chiropractors, salons)** | **Blocked at most sites unless the fire marshal agrees** | Knoxville adopted the 2024 International Fire Code (City Code § 11-21) and Knox County the 2018 edition. IFC § 609 sends "hyperbaric facilities" to NFPA 99, and IBC § 425 to NFPA 99 chapter 14, which soft chambers generally cannot meet. Minnesota's fire marshal applies this to mild chambers, Raleigh, Charlotte and Durham shut down mild-chamber wellness services in 2022, and an NFPA proposal to carve out low-pressure chambers failed 0 to 17 in 2023. No Knoxville ruling exists yet; the Knoxville Fire Marshal's answer decides this (open questions 4 to 7 in the federal/local memo). The HME licence does not apply to business placements, but renting Rx devices to a business may be "wholesale distribution" needing a Board of Pharmacy licence (open). |
| **Federal layer, both states** | Blocker in principle, rarely enforced | The cleared soft chambers (K072757, K220290) are prescription-only, cleared for acute mountain sickness and inflated with air; the concentrators are prescription-only; no reviewed clearance covers oxygen inside the chamber. Renting with no practitioner's order removes the Rx labelling exemption (21 CFR 801.109) and promoting wellness use is a new intended use (21 CFR 801.4). FDA's general-wellness policy (revised Jan 2026) is for low-risk products and FDA actively regulates chambers, so the "not a medical device" disclaimer probably does not reach. No FDA or FTC action against a soft-chamber renter was found 2023 to 2026; FDA's August 2025 letter stresses the fire risk of high-concentration oxygen. This applies in Utah as much as in Tennessee. |

## Requirements to handle (not blockers)
- **Registrations and tax.** Foreign-LLC certificate of authority ($300 minimum), TNTAP registration for sales and use tax and business tax, franchise and excise tax, and $15 city and county business licences once there is a local base. Rentals are taxable at 7% plus 2.25% (Knox County, Knoxville, Farragut) or 2.75% (Blount, Anderson). The medical-equipment exemption needs a prescription. How the single-article cap applies to monthly payments is inconsistent in state guidance.
- **Claims.** Tennessee's practice-of-medicine definition covers anyone who "professes to treat" an ailment (§ 63-6-204), and the FTC requires competent scientific evidence for health claims. Keep everything at relaxation and comfort. A "therapy" label at a host business can also get it reclassified as a medical clinic under Knoxville zoning.
- **Waivers.** Adult releases are enforceable case by case (*Copeland*, Tenn. 2018) but never for gross negligence; a parent cannot waive a child's claims (*Childress*, *Blackwell*), and a Utah choice-of-law clause may be ignored. Protection for minors and schools comes from insurance and use rules, not the waiver.
- **Base and zoning.** A Knoxville home base works if stock stays indoors and vans do not stage there; unincorporated Knox County is stricter. Warehousing is allowed in the I-MU, I-RD, I-G and I-H districts.
- **Auto-renewal.** Tennessee's 2024 automatic-renewal notice rule may apply to month-to-month continuation; state the renewal and the cancellation method plainly in every agreement.

## No issue found
State fire code (no hyperbaric mention), DOT hazmat for electric concentrators, OSHA beyond the general-duty clause, Knox County Health Department (no wellness-equipment program), 2023 to 2026 Tennessee legislation (only a resolution supporting veteran HBOT funding), and local news or enforcement.

## What would change the answer
1. An air-only configuration for business placements, if the Knoxville Fire Marshal confirms that a chamber without oxygen enrichment is not a "hyperbaric facility" under § 609 (question 5 in the federal/local memo).
2. A practitioner-order pathway (for example a telehealth order per customer), which would satisfy the federal Rx rule and possibly the HME record rules, at the cost of the no-prescription positioning.
3. Holding a Tennessee HME licence for home rentals, if counsel concludes the licence is required and obtainable for a wellness model.
4. Sourcing equipment whose clearance covers the intended use, if any exists.
