# Adversarial critique of the outreach emails (2026-10-03)

Read as the owner on the receiving end, before the first send. Findings that were fixed in the generator are marked Fixed; the rest are judgment calls left to the sender.

## Every template

- **The client's "why" was missing.** Fixed. Each email said who books and never why a member would spend $79 on an hour in a chamber. Added a "why" paragraph under "Who books, and why": the proof of demand (what Lehi, Orem and Salt Lake studios charge and that the owner's own clients are the ones paying), what the hour is like, and when it fits the routine. No health claims.
- **No credibility.** Fixed. "Purebaric places chambers in Utah businesses" claimed a track record the business line does not have yet and said nothing an owner could check. Replaced with what is true and checkable: a Lehi company already renting these chambers to homes across the Wasatch Front, owner-run, the owner installs and services. Services moved out of the paragraph and into the list, so nothing is said twice.
- **The $6,686 hero number invites "says who."** Fixed. Four sessions a day, every open day, is the moderate case in the prospectus but reads as the promise in a cold email. The hero tile now states the two-a-day floor ($2,853) beside it, the break-even tile says "about one every other day you are open," and the CTA says the assumptions are in the prospectus so they can swap in their own.
- **Three asks in the CTA.** Fixed. Call, reply, and visit the website competed. Now one ask, phrased as a question ("Worth 15 minutes to see if it fits [Business name]?"), with Reply as the primary button and Call as the secondary.
- **Staff-time objection unaddressed.** Fixed. "My front desk is already slammed" is the first thing a gym or salon owner thinks. Added "What it asks of you: a 5 x 8 ft spot, a wall outlet, and about 15 minutes of staff time per session," the prospectus figure.
- **Revenue in the subject line.** Fixed. "One chamber, about $6,700 a month in net revenue" from an unknown sender is a spam pattern. Subjects now lead with a question or a specific fact; the only number left is the break-even count.
- **Length.** Your call. The body runs 450 to 500 words, long for cold email, but each block answers a specific objection and the structure mirrors the business page as asked. The README notes a short-form option using the `.txt` opener and CTA.

## Gyms and fitness centers

- "Members recovering between workouts and sore from a new program" edged toward a soreness claim and described nobody in particular. Fixed: members who already spend on recovery (massage, sauna, compression boots), competitors and masters athletes between hard weeks, PT clients whose trainer builds recovery in.
- The owner's motive was implied, not stated. Fixed: revenue that is not dues, and a reason to come in on rest days.

## CrossFit and performance gyms

- Owners of boxes do not have spare square footage; the email never acknowledged it. Fixed: "without adding a coach, a class, or square footage you do not have," and the 5 x 8 footprint stays in the tiles.
- Added deloads and the Open as the moments a session fits, which is how these members actually schedule recovery.

## Sports teams and athletic programs

- **Wrong obstacle.** The draft pitched price to organizations for which $979 is a rounding error. The real blocker is procurement and sports-medicine sign-off, and the real alternative is a clinic across town or a six-figure hard-shell chamber. Fixed: the opener and headline now lead with "no capital purchase," "a monthly rental your department can approve," and "no maintenance contract."
- "Coaches and staff" as a who-uses-it bullet was filler. Fixed: "athletes you currently send to a clinic across town for sessions," which is a line item they recognize.
- The NBA and MLS first teams on the prospect list likely already own hard-shell chambers. Your call: lead with the academies, the Bees, Weber State and UVU, and with second facilities for the big programs. The README says so.
- Added the not-a-medical-device line to the fit paragraph, because an athletic trainer will ask before a front office does.

## Recovery studios and med spas

- "Members looking for the next thing to try" framed the chamber as a novelty. Fixed: members who would make it their weekly session.
- The strongest hook was buried in bullet three. Fixed: the first subject line is now the question "How often does someone ask [Business name] for hyperbaric?" and the opener says those clients are asking.
- An owner who has priced chambers will think "I could buy one." Fixed: "without buying a chamber, servicing a machine, or guessing whether it will sell." Purchase prices are not in the repo, so none are claimed.
- "Utah studios publish $79 to $129" now also says the estimate uses $75, below the local single rate, which preempts "your numbers assume premium pricing."

## Physical therapy, chiropractic, sports medicine

- Most skeptical reader of the six. The draft told them it is not a medical treatment but did not show them where it sits in a practice. Fixed: names the cash-pay wellness lines clinics already run (compression boots, red light, massage) and tells them to have counsel review the templates and confirm insurance coverage, which the prospectus already advises. Hiding that would cost trust on the call.
- "Outside the insurance billing cycle" was vague. Fixed: "no claims, no codes," and "revenue that does not depend on a payer," which is the actual appeal to a clinic owner.
- "Patients who finish a plan of care and want a reason to keep coming in" is the strongest retention point for PT and was kept.

## Salons and beauty studios

- Headline promised "every appointment." Fixed: "your regulars would add to their appointment."
- The owner's real objection is "who runs it while I am mid-fill, and where does it go." Fixed: "nothing for your artists to perform" in the opener plus the 15-minute staff line.
- The client's why was absent, and this is the audience whose why is least obvious. Fixed: the hour for themselves, and for some clients the early skin research, named only as something the client pamphlet summarizes with citations and limits. The email itself makes no skin claims.
- Your call: salons often open five days, so the 26-day month in the estimate is generous for them. At 22 days and four a day the net is about $5,500. The floor figure in the hero tile covers this; mention it on the call.
